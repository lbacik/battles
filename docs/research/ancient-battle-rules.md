# Ancient battle rules for a custom 2D application

Review date: **22 October 2026**. Sources: designers, publishers, official rulebooks, and legislation. This is a review to help choose a project direction, rather than a complete implementation specification or a legal opinion.

## Answer and options for PÓLIS

**Yes — there are many well-developed systems. Hoplite, Basic Impetus, and Triumph! are the most promising options for the current project.** They allow a unit to be represented as a map object with a front, flanks, and a defined tactical role. A 2D view suits both rectangular miniature bases and hex-based counters. It does not, however, provide a ready-made model for variable numbers of ranks and files.

The current [PÓLIS prototype](../../prototype/app.js) defines soldier count, files, ranks, spacing, and facing, and supports movement planning before simultaneous turn execution. Most of the rules below operate on whole units or bases; miniatures are not individually maneuvered soldiers. **None of the systems reviewed is a ready-made engine for this WEGO model.** The fit assessments in the table and the recommendations below are design inferences from comparing these models.

No open license was confirmed that would allow the text of any of the rulebooks described here to be freely incorporated into the application. **A free PDF provides access to read it; it does not grant a license to publish its text, illustrations, or a digital adaptation.** Practical paths include original rules inspired by the mechanics or an agreed license for a specific system.

## What PÓLIS can use in practice

**1. Hoplite as the primary reading on formations and cohesion.** Useful sections include §6.4 (phalanx maneuvers), §6.5 (columns), §9.6 (deep Theban phalanxes), §9.7 (shield wall), §9.8 (open order), and §10 (cohesion and rout). It is not an arbitrary formation generator: Theban depth is a specific unit type, and open order has a limited scenario context. The designers explicitly mark parts of the model, including the shield wall, as interpretation and speculation. This is a source of design ideas, not evidence that every mechanic is historically accurate. [Current rulebook](https://gmtwebsiteassets.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/hoplite/Hoplite2ndPrintRulesFinal.pdf).

**2. Basic Impetus as a simpler model for battles on a free-movement map.** It has few units and a shared parameter describing combat value and resilience. Its advantage for the prototype is the small set of states needed to show combat results. Frontage, depth, and fatigue can be designed separately, without incorporating the entire rulebook. [2008 rulebook](https://www.dadiepiombo.it/pdf/basiceng.pdf).

**3. Triumph! and DBM as models for command costs.** Moving a cohesive group and maneuvering distant units independently can have different costs. This may create more interesting decisions than allowing every possible order to be issued to every unit without a tradeoff. [Triumph! QRS](https://wgc-qrs.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/triumph-qrs-gameplay-v1-2.pdf), [DBM, p. 16](https://www.wrg.me.uk/WRG.net/History/DBM%203.2.pdf).

**Proposed direction for an original ruleset:** retain the free-movement 2D map and simultaneous orders; add a limited set of formation rules and clear cohesion, fatigue, and morale states. The first version only needs movement, rotation, formation changes, holding position, shooting, and attacking. Depth should provide a benefit at the cost of frontage or maneuverability, rather than acting as a free strength multiplier.

WEGO requires original decisions about units passing each other, the moment of contact, mutual charges, shooting during movement, blocked paths, and the order of routs. Replacing alternating activation with simultaneous movement changes the balance; it is more than a change in turn presentation. This is a design inference for the current prototype.

## Mechanics and content — a practical distinction

In Poland, Article 1(2¹) of the Copyright Act distinguishes protected expression from ideas, procedures, methods, and principles of operation. Article 2(4) distinguishes an inspired work from a derivative work, while Article 2(2) ties use of a derivative work to the rights holder's permission. Article 3 may protect a creative selection or arrangement of a collection. This supports distinguishing inspiration from mechanics from copying a rulebook, but does not automatically settle the status of a particular digital adaptation, set of tables, or army-list database. [Sejm text, version dated 16 October 2026, Articles 1–3](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/download.xsp/WDU19940240083/U/D19940083Lj.pdf).

The [U.S. Copyright Office — Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html) explains a similar distinction in the United States: an idea and method of play are separate from potential protection for rulebook text and artwork. This is a U.S. source, rather than an independent basis for assessing the project in Poland.

The practical choice for this application is **original code, original rule descriptions, original illustrations, and an original parameter set**, informed by deliberate inspiration. If the goal is a product marketed as a digital version of a specific system, the rights to that adaptation need to be established separately; buying a book or downloading a PDF does not establish them.
